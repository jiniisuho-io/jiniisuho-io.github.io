Architecture Is the Management of Trade-offs

Architecture is the disciplined management of engineering trade-offs.

The strongest architecture is not the most complex circuit. It is the simplest structure that delivers adequate margin under real product constraints.

A production-ready architecture must remain effective across realistic channel, PVT, timing, layout, power, control, and product operating conditions. A solution that only works at one corner, or only in simulation, is not yet an architecture — it is an idea.

What this means in practice

My focus is translating system-level requirements into robust circuit architectures, then maintaining architecture intent through implementation, validation, product correlation, and reusable methodology.

Technical leadership means improving the quality and repeatability of engineering decisions across the organization — so the next design starts from a stronger baseline than the last one.
