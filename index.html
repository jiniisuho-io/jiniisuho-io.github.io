Selected Architectures

Three problems solved at the architecture level

Silicon-validated examples of returning to fundamentals, identifying an exploitable design variable, and translating the resulting insight into production-ready architecture.

01

Global Current-Bias Architecture

Multi-voltage robustness through current-domain biasing

Challenge

Supply-dependent bias behavior

A voltage-based bias scheme created increased current variation and performance degradation as supply voltage decreased, increasing tuning requirements across operating conditions.

Architectural Insight

Move the reference to the current domain

Reduce direct dependency on the supply rail by distributing a current-domain reference rather than relying on a voltage-domain bias relationship.

Ownership
  • Quantified transient bias-fluctuation risk.
  • Derived decoupling requirements by buffer type.
  • Established current-bias placement guidance.
  • Led feasibility verification and silicon bring-up.
Result
  • Improved setup and hold margin behavior.
  • Reduced current under selected high-voltage conditions.
  • Preserved performance across supported supply conditions.
Scope of Impact

The central concept originated through team collaboration. My primary ownership covered feasibility analysis, quantitative risk assessment, implementation guidance, silicon verification, and progression toward product adoption.

Robust Multi-Voltage Behavior
Verified On Silicon
Scaled To Successor Products
02

EQ Margin Architecture

Receiver margin improvement without additional power or circuit complexity

Challenge

Conventional fixes created another problem

Increasing bias current would raise power while adding circuit blocks or control logic would increase implementation complexity.

Architectural Insight

Treat the gain-current trade-off as a design lever

Mapping gain, current, and margin relationships exposed a design variable that could be exploited through equalization instead of additional hardware.

First-Principles Approach
  • Analyzed fundamental receiver operation.
  • Mapped gain, current, and voltage-margin interdependencies.
  • Identified an exploitable degree of freedom.
  • Developed a minimum-complexity compensation concept.
  • Validated the concept during silicon bring-up.
Result
Improved Voltage Margin
Zero Additional Bias Current
Zero New Circuit Blocks

My Engineering Method

Different problems. One repeatable process.

A disciplined approach to identifying the real constraint, finding exploitable design space, and selecting the simplest architecture that produces measurable product value.

01

Analyze Fundamentals

Start from device, circuit, timing, and system behavior.

02

Find the Degree of Freedom

03

Choose the Simplest Architecture

Add complexity only when the additional complexity creates measurable product value.

04

Verify on Silicon

05

Productize and Scale

Convert solutions into constraints, guidelines, evaluation frameworks, and architecture baselines.

About

Architecture is the disciplined management of engineering trade-offs

A production-ready architecture must remain effective across realistic channel, PVT, timing, layout, power, control, and product operating conditions.

“The strongest architecture is not the most complex circuit. It is the simplest structure that delivers adequate margin under real product constraints.”

Technical leadership means improving the quality and repeatability of engineering decisions across the organization.

I am a Principal-level memory interface engineer with more than 20 years of experience across advanced DRAM, HBM, LPDDR, and NAND-related interface development.

My background covers architecture definition, analog and mixed-signal circuit design, receiver power optimization, PVT analysis, signal-margin evaluation, failure diagnosis, silicon validation, and product bring-up.

My focus is translating system-level requirements into robust circuit architectures, then maintaining architecture intent through implementation, validation, product correlation, and reusable methodology.
